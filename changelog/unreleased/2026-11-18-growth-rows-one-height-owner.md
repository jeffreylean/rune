- A growth stack that is itself a row of another growth stack draws in the slot the outer stack gives it, on every frame and whatever stacks came and went before. Its height and its top were each written twice per frame, once as a row and once as a stack, and the stack table's iteration order picked the winner, so the same tree could draw a nested stack at its final place while the row above it still opened, or a fraction of a point taller or shorter far down a long list. A nested stack now keeps its own track's height and the outer stack's offset, plus a virtual list's shift. Contents sliding with their row are applied after every stack is placed. A stack no other stack places still presents its rows' heights.
